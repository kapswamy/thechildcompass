When to seek urgent care

Trust your instinct.
Don’t wait in doubt.

Seek urgent medical attention for:

  • Breathing difficulty or bluish lips
  • Very sleepy, difficult to wake, or unresponsive child
  • Seizures, severe dehydration, or repeated vomiting
  • Persistent/worsening fever or sudden deterioration

Parent Pearl · Nutrition

Iron matters: feed the growing brain

Iron supports healthy red blood cells, brain development, energy and immunity during childhood.

Helpful iron-rich foods

Vegetarian: green leafy vegetables, pulses and beans, ragi, sprouts, sesame, and iron-fortified foods.

Non-vegetarian: egg, chicken, fish and liver.

Make it count

  • Offer a variety of iron-rich foods across the week.
  • Pair meals with vitamin-C foods such as orange, amla, guava or lemon.
  • Avoid tea, coffee and cola with meals, as they can reduce iron absorption.
Ask your pediatrician if your child looks unusually pale, tired, has poor appetite, or you are concerned about nutrition.

Parent Pearl · Newborn care

A tiny screen. A lifelong impact.

Newborn hearing screening is quick, safe and painless. It helps identify hearing concerns early, even when a baby appears perfectly healthy.

The 1-2-3 pathway

By 1 month: complete newborn hearing screening.

By 2 months: arrange diagnostic testing if the screen needs follow-up.

By 3 months: begin early support if hearing loss is confirmed.

Why it matters

Hearing supports speech, language, learning and social development. Early identification gives children the best chance to receive timely support.

Every newborn should be screened. Babies with a NICU stay, family history of hearing loss, certain infections or very low birth weight may need extra follow-up.

Parent Pearl · Development

When should I ask about speech?

Development is individual, but early questions are always worth discussing. A prompt assessment can help identify the right next step.

Some signs to discuss

  • By 12 months: not babbling, responding to their name, or using gestures.
  • By 18 months: not using single meaningful words or understanding simple commands.
  • By 24 months: not combining two words or following simple instructions.

What to do

  • Do not wait and watch if you are concerned.
  • Discuss hearing, development and the home language environment.
  • Ask whether assessment by a speech-language professional is appropriate.
Important: loss of any language or social skill at any age should be discussed promptly with a clinician.
